## From queues to hybrids

Scaled human moderation is breaking under its own volume. What's replacing it
almost everywhere is a hybrid still being improvised: AI-assisted triage,
human judgement on the hard cases, and a labelling regime that only works if
creators comply voluntarily.

## YouTube's actual policy, read closely

- creators self-disclose "AI use" during upload — yes or no
- **realistic** synthetic content needs disclosure; non-realistic content or
  minor edits don't
- YouTube can apply the label automatically and will notify the creator when
  it does
- elections, health and major public events get an extra, more prominent
  label

---

{/* _class: quote */}

If a viewer could reasonably think it really happened, it needs a label.

---

## Where this rule gets hard

- satire that's *meant* to be believed for a second before the joke lands
- research or journalism that reconstructs a real event to study it
- a consenting public figure licensing a synthetic version of their own voice

None of these are "realistic and deceptive" in the sense the rule was written
for, and all three get real edge-case emails to real trust and safety teams.
