## Week 2 — SIFT and lateral reading

# Leave the tab.

## The four moves

- **S**top — before you react, before you share
- **I**nvestigate the source — who is saying this, and why would they know?
- **F**ind better coverage — what do other, more reliable sources say?
- **T**race claims, quotes and media — back to where they actually originated

---

{/* _class: quote */}

A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention.

— Herbert Simon, 1971

---

## Why this reframing works

Most advice says: think harder about the thing in front of you. SIFT says:
stop looking at it and go check somewhere else first. None of the four moves
require you to be smarter than whoever made the claim.

---

## Lateral reading

Professional fact-checkers barely read the page they're checking. They open
new tabs and read *about* the source before reading *from* it. Reading
"vertically," staying on the page, is exactly what a well-engineered forgery
is built to survive.

---

## "Critically ignoring"

Not every claim deserves your attention. Deciding *not* to spend time on
something — on purpose, for a stated reason — is a skill in its own right,
not a failure to engage.

## Where SIFT runs out

SIFT tells you where a claim came from. It doesn't tell you whether an image
was generated from nothing, or whether a watermark survived a screenshot.
That's weeks 4 through 7.
