# Over 10 billion pieces of content. One company's watermark.

---

## SynthID, briefly

Google DeepMind's watermarking system embeds an imperceptible signal directly
into AI-generated images, audio, text and video — across Gemini, Imagen,
Lyria and Veo. Since launching at I/O 2023, it's watermarked more than 10
billion pieces of content, and the text method is open-sourced for anyone to
use.

---

## Deliberate, not accidental

Unlike a stylistic tell, a watermark is designed in on purpose:

- the generator nudges statistics only its own detector recognises
- invisible to a viewer, in principle recoverable by the matching tool
- Google states the SynthID watermark is designed to survive trimming, noise,
  compression, cropping and filtering

---

{/* _class: quote */}

"Designed to survive" is a claim about intent. Whether it actually does, for
your specific file, after three re-uploads, is an empirical question.

---

## Where it gets harder

Independent research through 2025 kept finding that watermarking and
provenance signals — however well engineered — can be weakened, removed, or
forged with surprisingly ordinary tools. A watermark surviving a lab test and
a watermark surviving a screenshot, a crop and a re-compress are two
different claims.

# 100% detection. Then 21%.

---

## UnMarker vs SynthID

- a disclosed research attack, reported through Google's own Vulnerability
  Reward Program — and acknowledged by Google
- one diffusion "purification" pass regenerates the image through a
  generative model, disturbing the watermark without visibly ruining the
  picture
- reported result: detection fell from roughly 100% to about 21% — a 79%
  attack success rate

---

{/* _class: quote */}

Google acknowledging an attack is not the same as the watermark being broken
for everyone, always. Read what the attack actually required before you
believe either headline.

## Three different claims, often confused for one

- **watermarking** — a signal deliberately embedded at creation
- **detection** — inferring machine origin from an artefact after the fact
- **provenance** (next week) — a signed record of the file's whole history
