# Slop at Work

## Week 9

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# $186 a month. Per person. Per office.

---

## The study

Stanford Social Media Lab and BetterUp surveyed 1,150 full-time US workers in
2025:

- **40%** had received "workslop" — AI-generated content that looks finished
  but isn't — in the past month
- workers estimated **15%** of what they receive qualifies
- dealing with the aftermath took an average of **1 hour 56 minutes** each
  time

---

## The math

$186 a month in lost time per affected worker. Scaled to a 10,000-person
organisation at the study's reported prevalence: over **$9 million a year**
in invisible productivity loss.

---

## It costs the sender too

Roughly half of respondents rated a colleague who sent workslop as less
creative and less capable than before. 42% saw them as less trustworthy. 37%
saw them as less intelligent. Nobody had to be caught lying for the
relationship to take the hit.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

Generating something that merely looks finished no longer costs any real
effort. That's the whole mechanism.

---

## Why this is harder than public slop

You're not unmasking a stranger's fraud. You're naming a colleague's
shortcut, inside a relationship you keep working within tomorrow. The
forensic skills transfer; the social skill of saying so kindly does not come
free.
