# Every reliable tell has a half-life.

---

## Tells that already decayed

- an odd fondness for "delve" — spiked hard in scientific abstracts right
  after ChatGPT launched, then got trained away once everyone noticed
- paragraphs arriving in tidy threes
- a habit of closing with "in conclusion"

None of these survive contact with a model released six months later.

---

## What's held up longer

- **perplexity** — how "surprised" a language model is by its own next word
- **burstiness** — human writing swings between simple and complex; generated
  text tends to flatten toward a single register
- hedging patterns that repeat across a whole document rather than varying
  naturally

---

{/* _class: quote */}

ChatGPT is a bullshit generator.

— Arvind Narayanan, Princeton, 2023

---

## Why "bullshit," precisely

Narayanan meant it as a diagnosis, not an insult, borrowing directly from
Harry Frankfurt's distinction: a liar cares what's true and hides it; a
bullshitter — and a next-token predictor — doesn't track truth at all.

---

## The false-positive problem

A distinctive, idiosyncratic human voice can look "generated" to a naive
detector, because both are unusual relative to average prose. This isn't a
footnote — it's the entire subject of week 11.
