# Stop guessing. Start signing.

## Six weeks of inference

Every method so far works backwards from the artefact: given only the file
itself, infer whether it was likely made or altered by a machine. That's
detective work — necessary, and permanently uncertain.

---

## A different idea

The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) records the truth
**at the moment of creation**, cryptographically signed, so nobody downstream
has to guess:

- who or what made the file
- which tools touched it, and when
- what was edited, and what the edit changed

---

## The same idea as a criminal chain of custody

Not proof of innocence — an unbroken, tamper-evident record of who held the
evidence and when. A gap in the chain doesn't prove guilt either. It just
means you're back to inference.

## What a valid credential can tell you

- a signed creation and edit history, when one was attached
- which tool or service produced the file

## What it can't

- that the *absence* of a credential means the file is fake — most content in
  the world was never signed at all
- that a signed history is itself immune to tampering upstream of the signature
