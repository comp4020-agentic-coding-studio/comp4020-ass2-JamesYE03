## The brief

> Find a real, current case where whether something is AI-generated actually
> matters to somebody — a disputed image, a suspicious workplace report, a
> platform's labelling decision — and investigate it properly: multiple
> methods, a documented chain of reasoning, and a recommendation.

This is the capstone, and it is deliberately the least scaffolded piece of
work in the course: no assigned passage, no assigned side, no assigned
methods. The room it leaves is entirely in the choice of case and the
judgement about which of the eleven weeks' methods that case actually calls
for — the strongest responses read as an investigation shaped by its subject,
not a syllabus applied to whatever subject was nearest to hand.

## What you submit

A public-facing investigative report (1200–1800 words), written for a reader
who was never in this course: the case, why it matters, every method applied
and what it found, a stated confidence level, and a concrete recommendation —
to a platform, an institution, or a reader — that the evidence actually
supports. A five-minute version of the same report is presented in week 12's
casework session before the written version is due.
