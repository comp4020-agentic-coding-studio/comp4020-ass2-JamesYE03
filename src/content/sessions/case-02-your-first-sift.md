The hardest part of SIFT is not any individual step — it's resisting the urge
to skip straight to step four, tracing the claim back, because that's the
satisfying one. Stop and Investigate are boring and they're where most of the
method's power actually lives.

## Before the session

Find one claim currently circulating that you have an opinion about but
haven't checked. Bring the link or screenshot, not your opinion.

## In the session

Working in pairs, run all four moves against the clock: two minutes per step,
timed. You will want more time on step four. You don't get it. The constraint
is the point — SIFT is designed to work under exactly the time pressure that
makes people skip it.

## Afterwards

Post your trace — what you found at each step, including dead ends — to the
week's discussion thread. A documented dead end is more useful to your
classmates than a suspiciously tidy success.
