Most advice about online misinformation tells you to think harder about the
thing in front of you. Mike Caulfield's SIFT method does the opposite: it
tells you to stop looking at the thing in front of you and go look somewhere
else first. **Stop. Investigate the source. Find better coverage. Trace the
claim to its origin.** None of these four moves require you to be smarter than
whoever made the claim — they require you to leave the tab.

That reframing matters more than ever now that the "thing in front of you" can
be generated to specification. A forged claim can be engineered to survive
your close reading, because your close reading is exactly what it was
optimised against. It is much harder to engineer a claim that survives being
checked against everything else that is or isn't said about it elsewhere.

## Outline

- the four moves of SIFT, and why order matters
- lateral reading: professional fact-checkers barely read the page they're
  checking
- "critically ignoring": the skill of not spending your attention on
  something, on purpose
