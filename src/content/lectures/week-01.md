In 2005 the philosopher Harry Frankfurt published a short, serious essay called
*On Bullshit*, arguing that bullshit is worse than lying because a liar at
least cares what the truth is. In 2020, two data scientists built an entire
undergraduate course — and later a bestselling book — out of that essay,
teaching students to spot bullshit dressed up as data. In 2023, a Princeton
computer scientist called a chatbot's confident nonsense "a bullshit
generator" and meant it as a precise diagnosis, not an insult.

This course sits at the next turn in that lineage. The bullshit used to be
handmade — a pundit reaching for a persuasive-sounding statistic, an ad
department reaching for a graph. It is now manufactured at a scale no human
liar could match, by systems with no stake in the truth at all, and it arrives
looking exactly as polished as the real thing. We call the residue **slop**,
and this course teaches you to work a case.

## Outline

- what changed between "bullshit" and "slop" — scale, intent, and confidence
- the forensic stance: evidence, chain of custody, and the discipline of not
  guessing
- what the next eleven weeks build toward: your own investigations, not just
  your own opinions

## Before you arrive

Read the syllabus end to end and bring one piece of content — a text, an
image, a clip — that you already suspect is synthetic. You will not be asked
to prove it yet. Just bring your hunch; week 2 is where hunches get held to a
standard.
