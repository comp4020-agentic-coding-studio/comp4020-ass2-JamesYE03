Everything so far has treated slop as a public, adversarial problem — someone
out there trying to fool someone else. "Workslop" is quieter and, in a way,
more corrosive: a 2025 Stanford/BetterUp study found that a large share of
knowledge workers had received AI-generated material at work in the past
month that looked complete but wasn't — a report with no real analysis behind
it, a summary that restated the brief instead of advancing it. Nobody was
trying to deceive anybody. The generating cost of looking finished had simply
dropped below the generating cost of actually finishing.

The forensic skills from earlier weeks still apply, but the stakes and the
social context are different: you are not unmasking a stranger's fraud, you
are naming a colleague's shortcut, inside a relationship you have to keep
working within tomorrow. This session is about that harder conversation as
much as it's about the detection.

## Outline

- what the research actually measured, and what it didn't
- why workslop is invisible to the sender and expensive for the receiver
